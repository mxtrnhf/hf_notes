# Why ships spin the way they do: engine thrust, the hidden hull stabiliser, and the lurch on swivel + static designs (v1.163), measured behaviour

Written 2026-10-21 from telemetry of Lightning variants (Shipworks test flights) plus the code reads in `hull-stabiliser-engine-logic.md` and `static-engine-force-path.md`. This note is the game-facing summary: what the player sees, the numbers, worked examples, what was ruled out, and what is still open. Tags: **[code]** read from the binary, **[data]** measured in flight, **[inference]** reasoned, not proven.

## 0. Overview (one page)
* **What it is.** Every engine part pushes the hull against its own spin and against its tilt, every physics step, through `Body_AddTorque` on the Frame (`creatureLogic` case 200). It runs while the ship thrusts and for 0.6 s after. It is a game mechanic ("anti-tilt", `m_mdl_at`), not rigid-body physics: it changes the hull's angular velocity directly, so it never shows in any force or torque accumulator.
* **Formula.** Per engine part: `Tw = -spin * I * 0.05 * frac`, `Ta = -wrapped_angle * I * 0.05 * frac`, `frac = m_at / (2 * min(bank sums of m_at))`, `I` = the Frame body alone about its origin. A thrust bank that applied no force in the tick turns the angle term off and halves the damping. `Tw` is zero once the thrust history (`+0x15b4`, about 30 ticks) is empty. `T` is capped at +-`m_at`.
* **What it does to each design.** Swivel-only: the cap hides the angle term, smooth plateau (7.15 rad/s on four swivels). Statics-only: one static never fires, so a bank is always idle, pure damping, plateau = engine torque / about 105 N*m per rad/s (0.3 rad/s with the statics near the centre, 1.6 rad/s spread wide). Swivels plus statics: the angle term is live and flips sign where the wrapped hull angle passes +-pi, a push of about 0.38 rad/s in one 20 ms step, once per turn (the lurch).
* **What it is not.** Not Box2D (step, solver, damping 0.05 are stock), not antennas, not contacts, not joint motors, not hull air drag, not the AI attitude hold (a separate system on the AI list), not an editor-only effect.
* **Who gets it.** The player ship and AI ships alike (the same case runs on the enemy ship's engines).
* **Geometry facts this needed.** The Frame body origin is the Frame node's position in the design file, not the file's coordinate origin (checked on 17 ships of 10 designs: COM offset, mass and inertia equal the logged values). Landing legs sit folded at their joint limits in flight (section 7c). The two MDL_EVAC parts are also free bodies (section 7b).
* **Open.** Why the response to the Frame's angular impulse after the joint solver shares it is best matched by about 0.75 of the whole body set's inertia and not by a single value across designs; behaviour on other ship classes, arcade and campaign; what zeroes the per-tick bank sums.

## 1. What you see in the game

| Design | Hold E or Q from rest | Release all keys |
|---|---|---|
| 4 swivel engines (Lightning, statics removed) | spin rises smoothly to a plateau of **7.15 rad/s** and stays there | spin falls to about half within **0.6 s**, then coasts at about 0.05 /s |
| 2 static engines only | spin rises to a constant rate: **0.3 rad/s** with the statics near the centre, **1.6 rad/s** with them spread wide | falls to about 40% within 0.6 s, then coasts |
| 4 swivel + 2 static (stock Lightning) | spin **lurches**: 1.5 to 4.5 rad/s, once per turn, a push just after the hull points straight down | same 0.6 s drop |
| 4 swivel + 2 static, statics wide | lurches, weaker than stock | same |

Plain-physics expectation (engines at the sides, a real rigid body): the spin keeps rising while the engines fire and the hull keeps its rate when they stop. None of the rows above does that. A rigid-body model of the same hulls without the stabiliser below ends at 13 rad/s and never drops after release.

## 2. The cause: a built-in per-engine stabiliser [code][data]

Every engine part calls `Body_AddTorque` on the hull's Frame every physics step (`creatureLogic` case logic id 200, return address `0x1400357d1`). It is not a force, so it never shows in a body's force or torque accumulators. `AddTorque` changes the angular velocity at once: `w += T * 0.05^5 / I_com(Frame)` (checked: measured jump 0.0145 vs 0.0146 predicted, within 1.2%, 0.1% and 0.3% on three designs).

```
gate = creature+0x180c = 2 * min(bank-1 sum, bank-2 sum of Stats+0x100 [m_mdl_at])
frac = m_at(part) / gate
I    = Frame body alone, about its own origin = (m*|lc|^2 + I_com) / 0.05^5      (game units)
Tw   = -w * I * 0.05 * frac                     (spin damping)
Ta   = -wrap(hull angle) * I * 0.05 * frac      (pull towards level, angle 0)
if a thrust bank applied no force this tick:  Ta = 0 and Tw *= 0.5
if creature+0x15b4 (thrust history, ~30 ticks) == 0:  Tw = 0
T = clamp(Tw + Ta, +-m_at)
```
Verified at register level by logging the stabiliser's own values on every call: the logged `Tw` equals `-w * I * 0.05 * frac` with ratio 1.00000 and no scatter on about 3 100 (four swivels), 2 300 (two statics), 8 800 (stock) and 6 500 (wide statics + swivels) active rows. Where both terms are live and nothing is clamped (300, 8 460 and 2 916 rows) the logged total minus `Tw` equals `-a * I * 0.05 * frac` to the same precision. Enemy ships' calls follow the same formula (see section 6).

Properties that follow:
* **Position of the engine does not matter.** Each engine part contributes `m_at * u` whatever its place on the hull. A static near the centre gives the same stabiliser torque as one at the edge, but produces almost no turning torque.
* **Static and swivel engines both count**, in the gate and in the sum of calls. The gate is twice the weaker bank's total `m_at`.
* **It is active while the ship thrusts and for 0.6 s after.** `+0x15b4` is a rolling sum of recent thrust samples. After the last thrust it drains in a straight line and reaches zero 0.6 s later, and the case then sets `Tw = 0`. [data: drained from 6.7e7 to 0 over exactly the 0.6 s in which the torque stopped.]
* **Coasting afterwards is normal**: Box2D linear/angular damping is 0.05, set at body creation and never changed.
* **Arcade variant** [code]: if creature `+0xad4` > 0 and the arcade byte `0x147fc6fd4` is non-zero, `kd` becomes 0.02 and `kp` 0. All flights here had the normal 0.05 values.

### Worked example: four swivel engines at full effect
| Step | Value |
|---|---|
| `m_mdl_at` of one swivel engine (ENGINE_03/04) | 1e7 |
| Spin change per call: 1e7 * 0.05^5 * (1/68.45) | 0.0457 rad/s |
| Four engines, per 20 ms step | 0.183 rad/s = 9.1 rad/s^2 |
| As a torque, I * alpha (I about COM = 68.45 b2) | about 624 N*m |
| Engine thrust per engine 36e6 game units * 0.05^4 | 225 N, summed engine torque about 730 N*m |
So the stabiliser caps at about the same torque the engines make, and the spin stops climbing at the plateau.

## 2b. What the swivel engines do in a pure rotation command [data]
From the torque probe (every engine body's angle at 50 Hz, relative to the Frame), four-swivel Lightning:
* **E held from rest:** both right-bank engines (command codes 101/103) swing the same way, positive, from 0 to +pi in about 2.6 s (0.53, 0.97, 1.45, 1.90, 2.23, 2.66, 2.97, 3.10 rad at 0.06, 0.12, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4, 0.6, 0.9, 1.2 s) and then sit near +-pi. The left-bank engines (100/102) stay at 0 (0.00 to 0.28 rad after 3 s). The right bank therefore fires backwards and the left bank forwards.
* **Q held:** the roles swap. The right bank settles near 0 (it was at -1.55) and the left bank swings through -pi (negative, shortest path from -1.55) and ends near +-pi.
* A command exactly opposite to an engine (error = pi) turns it in the positive direction, so two engines at the same angle swing together; the servo law is `motor speed = wrap(cmd - angle) * power^2 * 3.0` (see `static-engine-force-path.md`).

## 3. Why each design behaves as it does [data + code]

* **Swivels only: smooth.** The braking `Tw` alone exceeds the cap `m_at` above about 2.9 rad/s, so `T = +-m_at` on 84% of active ticks. The angle term has amplitude at most `kp * pi * m_at = 1.1e7`, too small to pull `T` below the cap, so it is invisible. The plateau is where the capped stabiliser torque (about 624 N*m) matches the engine torque.
* **Statics only: smooth, any position.** One static sits in the wrong half-plane for a pure rotation command, so one thrust bank applies no force on every tick: the angle term is off (`Ta` = 0) and the damping is halved. What remains is pure damping `u = -0.226 * w`, so the plateau is the engine torque divided by about 105 N*m per rad/s. Statics near the centre make 31 N*m (plateau 0.3 rad/s), statics wide make 178 N*m (1.6 rad/s). The plateau follows the lever arm, as it would for a rigid body with simple drag.
* **Swivels plus statics: the lurch.** The gate is 7e7, so the gain is lower (0.21) and the braking is not at the cap. Both banks fire, so the angle term is live. `Ta = -0.20 * wrap(angle) * m_at` pulls the hull back towards level and flips sign where the wrapped angle jumps from +pi to -pi. At that point each swivel call's torque changes by about 1.2e7 in one step. The six calls (four swivels, two statics at 1.5x weight) add up to about 8.3e7, a push of 0.38 rad/s in 20 ms (about 19 rad/s^2). The spin climbs, is pulled back, then jumps once per turn. With the statics spread wide the lurch is weaker in flight (gain 0.23 against 0.21, Frame inertia about COM 81.6 against 70.9) but still there; why it weakens was not isolated [inference].
* **Why the lurch starts "just after pointing down".** Spin climbs against the level-seeking pull from angle 0 up to 180 degrees; past 180 degrees the same pull helps the rotation instead of resisting it.

Idle-bank statistics on the player's active ticks: four swivels 8.5%, two statics 100%, stock 4.1%, wide statics + swivels 7.7%.

## 4. What was ruled out [data]
* **Physics engine behaviour**: Box2D step, solver and damping are stock (see `box2d-physics-setup.md`). The only change in spin between one step's exit and the next step's entry is the `AddTorque` jump.
* **Antenna chains**: 24 welded ghost bodies (mass 1.25e-10) were tested; designs with the antennas deleted give the same plateau and the same lurch.
* **Contacts**: zero touching-contact rows in the constant-spin window.
* **Joint motors**: gun motors had a motor impulse of about 1e-5 in a 1.6 rad/s spin; leg joints under 0.2 N*s.
* **Hull air drag** (`Body_PreStepUpdate`): about 15 N*m at 50 u/s against 700 to 900 N*m of engine torque; real but irrelevant to rotation.
* **Engine force application**: `Body_AddForceAtPoint` applies each engine force correctly at the part centre; the engine joints pass 90 to 105% of it to the hull.
* **AI attitude hold** (`AiShip_SteerController`): a separate system run from the AI list only [code; one detail about list registration is unverified, see `player-attitude-stabiliser.md`]. It is not this stabiliser.

* **Checked and found consistent with the game's own numbers**: the per-bank engine force sums equal the logged ones to within 1% through a hold and through the swing of the right bank (e.g. 72.0 / 44.1 / 69.8 e6); the hull's drag and gravity reproduce the deceleration of a coast to a ratio of 1.00 over 0.3 s intervals at 92 to 94 u/s; the spin and position responses fit best with no delay between the logged keys and the forces (a lag of 0 +- 0.02 s on three sessions); the engines' starting angle decides the first half second of a window (with the logged angles the 2 s error is 2 to 3 u, with the engines at rest 21 u).

## 5. Consequences
* **Design guidance.** A ship's turn rate is set by two things only: the engines' turning torque (lever arm times thrust) and the stabiliser's cap `sum(m_at)`. The `m_mdl_at` values (OL.seria): ENGINE_01 and 02 7e7, ENGINE_03 and 04 1e7, ENGINE_05 1.5e7, ENGINE_06 4e6. More or heavier engines raise both the gate and the cap; the gain `0.05 * I / gate` falls as the gate rises. A big hull with a small gate has a large gain and saturates the cap.
* **To remove the lurch** on a swivel + static design [inference, not flown]: make it saturate (lower gate: fewer or lighter angular-thrust parts), or keep a bank idle in a rotation (statics on one side only).
* **Players' description** ("anti-tilt", `m_mdl_at`, "applies every frame while an engine produces thrust, regardless of where it is, remove it and the ship is uncontrollable") matches this note.
* **A test flight that would confirm** [not flown]: set `m_mdl_at` to 0 on the engines in OL.seria (back the file up first), fly the four-swivel Lightning, hold E for 5 s and release. Prediction from the rigid-body model: the spin runs to about 13 rad/s and, after release, coasts at about 0.05 /s with no 0.6 s drop. AI ships lose their stabiliser and their attitude hold (the gate comes from `m_at`), so fly with no enemies.

## 6. AI ships get it too [data]
The probe's calls from the enemy ship (gate 2e7, `m_at` 1e7 per call, I = 1.0e8 to 1.2e8) follow the same formula to five digits. AI ships therefore have this stabiliser on top of the steering attitude hold in `AiShip_SteerController`; any analysis of AI hull motion has to include both.

## 7. Open
* **Frame body origin** [resolved]: `I` uses the offset of the COM from the Frame body's origin. That origin is the Frame node's position in the design file (the bridge sits at the node's local (0, 0)), not the file's coordinate origin. With it, the COM offset computed from the part data is 0.335 / 0.327 / 0.350 / 0.335 m for the four Lightning variants against 0.334 / 0.327 / 0.350 / 0.335 m logged. The node's position is a non-zero point in the shared coordinates (for example (-3.63, 3.15) game units for the stock design); measuring from the coordinate origin instead gives 0.45 to 0.51 m on two of the designs and wrong gains. Checked on ten other designs from earlier battles (Archangel, Negev, Nimrod, Gladiator, Fearsome, Sarma, Flower, Navarin, Slogger, Courageous; 17 ships): the COM offset, Frame mass and inertia about the COM computed from the design with this origin equal the game's logged Frame values exactly; from the file's coordinate origin the offsets are wrong by 0.13 to 1.46 m.
* **Ship classes beyond Lightning**, and campaign or arcade battles. All flights were Lightning variants in Shipworks test flights. A thrust-scaling rule in `Engine_ApplyThrustForce` (factor `f(X)` gated to editor and test flights, see `static-engine-force-path.md`) was not needed for the rotation but may matter for linear motion.
* **How the solver redistributes the impulse** to engines, legs and guns that sit on their own joints in the steps after the Frame's jump. A model that lets the whole welded assembly absorb the impulse reproduces 14 flights; a sweep of that share from 0 to 1.3 has a flat optimum at 0.85 to 1.0. A direct measurement was too confounded by engine force. Not proven.
* **Lurch period on long runs**: the model's lurches come about 10% faster than the game's after a few turns, so the angle drifts over tens of seconds. Short windows (2 to 5 s) are accurate. The cause is not found.

## 7b. Side finding: MDL_EVAC parts are also free bodies [data]
In the torque probe's body list the two `MDL_EVAC` parts (slot type 1097, mass 17 911, b2 mass 2.239 each) appear as separate dynamic b2 bodies with no joint. They drift away from the hull from the first seconds (about 15 m at 2 s, 70 m at 30 s). The Frame body's mass (166.98 b2 on the stock Lightning) already includes the EVAC fixtures: leaving them out gives 162.51. So the pods exist in addition to their fixtures in the Frame. They never touch the ship and do not change its motion, but they count if a body list is summed as if it were the ship.

## 7c. Landing legs sit folded in flight [data]
In flight the landing legs are not in the pose the design file draws. For the stock Lightning (torque probe) each leg's angle relative to the Frame stays constant within 0.03 rad over 9 s while the hull spins at 2 to 4.5 rad/s, with joint angles of -1.97 to -1.78 rad at the joint limit. The design file draws each leg joint at one end of its range (joint angle 0) and the leg holds the other end (+-1.9635 rad, from `m_angle_min` / `m_angle_max`, the missing limit being 0) in flight, with each lower segment's hinge carried by its parent's fold. Folded, the leg centres of mass sit at about (+-0.54, 0.14) and (+-0.55, 0.10) m from the Frame's COM, against the logged (+-0.56, 0.14) and (-0.63 / 0.59, 0.10), where the drawn pose gives (+-0.8, 0.52) and (+-1.1, 0.92). The inertia of the whole body set (Frame, engines, guns, legs) about its COM is 89.9 in flight against 99.1 for the drawn pose. The legs are held at the limit, so for rotation they act as rigid.

## 8. Evidence
* Telemetry probe files: per-step torque and body states for the player's bodies, hook rows for every `AddTorque`, `AddForce*` and `SetAngularVelocity` call on the hull with the caller address, per-step creature fields (`+0x17fc`, `+0x1800`, `+0x15b4`, `+0x180c`, `+0xbc4`), per-engine-part rows, and the stabiliser's own register values. The enemy ship's calls appear in the same rows; they were filtered out before the numbers above.
* Flights: four-swivel (5), statics-only wide (3 plus one close), stock (5), wide statics + swivels (1), 14 sessions in the validation.
* Check of the formula against logged flights from key presses alone, no fitted gain, reset every 5 s, median per session: spin error 0.00 to 0.40 rad/s with the stabiliser against 2.6 to 10.5 without, hull angle error 0.05 to 1.3 rad. A held-out design (statics wide plus swivels) was predicted to 0.07 rad/s.
* Related notes: `hull-stabiliser-engine-logic.md` (the decompile), `static-engine-force-path.md` (force path and frame drag), `player-attitude-stabiliser.md` (AI attitude hold and who reads `+0x180c`), `box2d-physics-setup.md`, `box2d-struct-layouts.md`.

## 9. Code hunt for a hull-rotation term that depends on |w| (answer to simulator, 2026-10-22) [code]
Question: sim reproduces engines, servo, overheat, but the Frame spin is off when |w| > 1.5 rad/s (Lightning 1792617339 t 171.8-180.8 reaches -9.2 rad/s; the sim lags 2x in the first 2 s). Result: **no code bounds or changes the Frame's angular motion as a function of |w|.** Nothing was found; the list below is what was ruled out, with addresses.

**(a) Angular part of `Body_PreStepUpdate` 0x14001d440: there is none.** Full decompile read again. Frame branch = the linear quadratic drag only (`F = -vhat * A * min(W,H) * |v|^2 * 1.29 * 0.5` at the point `+0x210/+0x214`, see `static-engine-force-path.md` section 3). Its only angular effect is the torque of that force about the COM, which depends on the hull angle and `|v|`, not on `w`. After the if/else every body (Frame included) also gets: `+0x1e4` gravity-like `(0, -9.82 * mass * k)` and a wind-like `+0x1e8 * |d|^2` toward `(+0x1ec, 0)` with `d = (+0x1ec - vx, -vy)`, both at the centre (vtable `+0x178`, no torque); `k` (`+0x1e4`) and `+0x1e8` are 0 unless set, I did not read their values for a Frame. `Body+0x1e0` (`m_drag_ang`) is not read here. Box2D angular damping is the only `w`-linear loss: `b2Island_Solve` 0x140323be0, before the constraint solve: `w = (w + h * invI * T_accum) * clamp(1 - h * 0.05, 0, 1)` with `h` = 0.02, i.e. `w *= 0.999` per step (Box2D 2.3 form, not `1/(1+h*d)`), on every body including engines and guns.

**(b) Clamps.** `b2Island_Solve` after the solve: translation clamp 2.0 m per step (|v| > 100 m/s = 2000 u/s) and **rotation clamp pi/2 per step (|w| > 78.5 rad/s)**. Both are stock and out of reach at 9 rad/s. Sleep: a body sleeps only below |w| 2 deg/s (sq 0.00122) and |v| 0.01 m/s for 0.5 s. No world-level or game-level limit on w. Writers of b2 `w`: `b2Island_Solve`, `Body_AddTorque`, `Body_SetAngularVelocity` 0x140051760 (callers listed in `static-engine-force-path.md`: tear-off, fragments, debris, level script, hangar; none runs on the player Frame in flight).

**(c) Stabiliser gate, re-read instruction by instruction (0x140035652..0x1400357d1).** Order: skip if byte `0x143a11909` is set (named `doRecoil_global`; written 1 by `displayTextOnCreature?` 0x14023271d on screen transitions and 0 by `LocalFileReadAndUpdate` 0x140269cd3; `handlePauseUnpause` only reads it. An earlier version of this note called it the pause flag: wrong); `creature = part+0xb8`, test flag 0x2f on it; target body `RDI` = the creature's root body (`0x140048fc0`) if it has class flag 0xf, **else the part itself**; `frac = m_at / creature+0x180c` (0 if the gate is 0); `I = Body_GetInertiaAboutOrigin(RDI)`; `w = RDI+0x13c`, `a = wrap(RDI+0x138)`; `kd,kp` = 0.05 (0.02, 0 if `creature+0xad4 > 0` and arcade byte `0x147fc6fd4`); `Tw = -w*I*kd*frac`, `Ta = -a*I*kp*frac`; if `+0x17fc == 0 or +0x1800 == 0`: `Ta = 0`, `Tw *= 0.5`; if `+0x15b4 == 0`: `Tw = 0`; `T = Tw + Ta`, clamped to `+-m_at`; skip if dt global `0x1403c126c` is 0; `RDI.AddTorque(T)`. Facts for the sim:
* **No test of the part's own state**: the case does not read `+0x810` (lockout), `+0x818` (power) or `+0x848`. A locked-out, overheated or unpowered engine still brakes the hull with its full `m_at` share (only a destroyed or inactive part, `vtable +0x78` false, is skipped by `Part_UpdateDispatch`). The gate `+0x180c` and the `m_at` shares come from the stats pass, not from live thrust.
* **No |w| or key (Q/E) test anywhere.** The only |w|-dependent thing is the `+-m_at` cap on the sum (it bites at |w| > about 2.9 rad/s for four swivels, 3.1 at other gates: `u = -kd*I/gate*w`, `|u| > 1`).
* `w` and `a` are read from `+0x13c/+0x138`, which `Body_SyncFromB2` refreshes once per step; `AddTorque` changes the b2 `w` at once but not `+0x13c`, so all engine calls in one tick see the same `w` and each call adds its own `T` (no feedback within a tick).
* `+0x15b4` and `+0x17fc/+0x1800` react only to the thrust the engines applied (`g = cos(cmd - angle) > 0`), not to |w|. A swivel pointing 90 degrees off still counts; a bank is "idle" only when no engine of that bank applied force in the tick.

**(d) Servo and joints.** `Engine_ServoUpdate` 0x140053690 sets motor speed `d * P * Stats+0x108 * P` (linear, no limit, no clamp except the wrap of `d` to [-pi, pi] with the tie rule in `player-thrust-keys-and-engine-heat.md` section 2) and max torque `mass/100` through `Joint_SetMotor` 0x1401949d0 (stores `+0x10c` speed, `+0x108` torque, forwards to the b2 revolute joint when `Joint+0xd0 == 1`). The b2 motor speed is a **relative** speed (`wB - wA`), and `d = wrap(c - p)` uses the world angle of the part and the command `c` = hull angle (+ pi), so the joint angle `phi = p - a` obeys `phi' = 3 * wrap(delta - phi)` whatever `w` is: **the servo has no steady lag proportional to the hull spin**; only the one-tick staleness of `a` and `p` (both from the last `SyncFromB2`, so equal) and the motor impulse limit `maxTorque * dt` (300.76 * 0.02 = 6 against an engine inertia of 0.0894: not limiting). Swivel engine joints have **no angle limits** (`hull-stabiliser-coupling.md`); only the leg joints do.

**(e) Callers that put torque or force on a Frame, outside the engines and the stabiliser.** Direct call sites of vtable `+0x1a0` (AddTorque), full-image scan: `0x1400357cb` (the stabiliser), `0x1400359f1` (logic id 210 block, a self-torque with `-w` and angle terms), `0x140036197` (missile id 1/3/4 block), `0x140037286` and `0x140037a8b` (rocket PD, the latter in the id 2 block), `0x1400392bd/0x1400392d9` (rocket PD, `I * (kp*angle_err - kd*w)` clamped), `0x140060a49` (`FUN_140060a20`, forwards to the creature root Frame; vtable only, no code caller), `0x14000a308` (aircraft attitude PD), `0x1402be214` and `0x1402c07ed` (level-script region, not read). Every creatureLogic site acts on `RSI` = the logic body itself (a missile, rocket or projectile), not on the ship Frame. Vtable `+0x170` (AddForceAtPoint) in battle code: `Body_PreStepUpdate` 0x14001d716 (Frame drag), `Engine_ApplyThrustForce` 0x140028833, `creatureLogic` 0x140043b9c and 0x1400390f4 (on `RSI`, not read in full). Vtable `+0x178` (AddForceAtCenter) in creatureLogic: 0x14003482a, 0x1400359ad, 0x1400371e1, 0x140037a59 (rocket blocks), 0x14003ad54/ade4/ae20 (logic-id 820 block: the landing-support lift `(0, 3.0 * mass)` etc., on `RSI`, needs a linked creature `RSI+0x498`), 0x1400431df, 0x1400439fe (not read). The scan matches by displacement, so it also lists unrelated vtables outside battle code (map, strategic fleet): ignore addresses above 0x1400f0000 for ship physics.

**What remains plausible, in my order [inference, not proven]:**
1. **The stabiliser torque itself, not the rigid body.** The log has the stabiliser's own `AddTorque` rows (ret `0x1400357d1`). ### 9b. Where the Frame drag rectangle (`+0x210/+0x214/+0x218/+0x21c`) comes from (answer to simulator, 2026-10-22) [code]
* **Same rectangle as `Frame_RecomputeBounds` 0x14011cf60** (Body vtable `+0x1f8`). Instruction scan of every store to `+0x210..+0x21c` in ship code: the Frame's four fields are written only there (0x14011d2d2/2e8/2fb/309); `bodySecondaryInit` 0x140025485 and `Body_ComputeMeshBounds` 0x140054720 write the *child* mesh size at `+0x218/+0x21c` (and a centre at `+0x210/+0x214`) once at load; the other hits are unrelated classes.
* **Formula:** loop over the Frame's child list (`Frame+0x100`, entries with class nibble `& 0xf == 0xf`): per child, the rectangle `child+0x218 x child+0x21c` (its own mesh bbox size) centred on the child origin (`child+0x128/+0x12c`), rotated by the child matrix `child+0x8fc`, four corners; AABB over all corners in the Frame's local axes (not rotated by the hull angle). Then `Frame+0x210/+0x214` = AABB centre, `+0x218` = W (full width), `+0x21c` = H (full height), `+0x20c` = half diagonal (via Sqrt_Cache). Empty child list: all zero. If any child has density `+0x1d0 == 0` the Frame b2 body is made static (type 0), else dynamic (2).
* **Which parts count:** only parts in the Frame's `+0x100` child list. Swivel engines and guns have their own b2 body (their master is the global node) so they are not in that list; static engines, hull plates, trusses and everything welded to the Frame are. A swivel engine turning therefore does not change the drag rectangle.
* **When recomputed:** not per tick. The vtable-`+0x1f8` call sites that can reach a ship Frame are `CreateFrameFragments` 0x14011c1a0 (0x14011c40c/0x14011c419; also through its helper 0x14011bdd0) and Frame creation / mesh rebuild (`handleBodyMesh?` 0x14001c300, `New` 0x14001db50); none is in `creatureLogic`, `Part_Tick`, `SomethingPhysicTask` or the damage path I scanned. So a part destroyed without a Frame split leaves W,H,P as they were at the last recompute (spawn or last break-up). Not proven for every indirect caller (the scan matches by displacement and includes unrelated classes).
* **Sim consequence:** compute W,H,P once at spawn from the static parts of the design (not the swivel engines, not the guns), keep them fixed until a break-up; the child sizes come from `Body_ComputeMeshBounds` (each part's mesh bbox). If the sim lets the rectangle include swivels or follow engine angles it over-sizes the drag and moves its torque centre.
